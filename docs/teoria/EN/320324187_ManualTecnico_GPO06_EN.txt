1. Objective and scope
Develop and document an interactive 3D environment for Windows that connects planetary exploration,
racing, flight and observation. The individual contribution covers the Rek’Sai avatar, its hierarchical
animation, input capture and HUD, integrated with the team’s engine and world.
The implemented scope includes a space base and three surfaces: rock, forest and ocean. Zone
transitions change state and scene; they are not continuous landings on a sphere. F1-F4 select
experiences. Follow, chase, flight and orbit camera rigs determine how each experience is viewed.
Limitations
Window::Initialise requests an OpenGL 4.3 Core context, although earlier documentation referred to 3.3.
The target is Windows x64 with C++17 and toolset v143. There is no biological simulation or general
physics engine. Delivery textures were reduced to at most 512 × 512, which reduces visual detail. No
performance benchmark or independent second-computer test is available.
Technical background
Programmable rasterization separates vertex attributes, transformations, texture sampling and
per-fragment lighting [1]. The project uses this pipeline for imported models and procedural geometry.
GLFW manages the window, context and events [2]; GLM supplies vector and matrix types [3]; Assimp
reads model files into an imported scene [4]. Animation uses hierarchical transforms, parametric paths
and keyframes, making the mathematics of each frame explicit.

Data, ownership and ordering
InputCapture updates held keys, press edges, mouse motion and wheel input. The delivery fix preserves
presses released within one glfwPollEvents call and consumes each event once. CameraDirector mode
and active zone are independent states. Normal frame time is capped at 0.05 s; animation tests use a
fixed 1/60 s step.
SceneNode owns children through unique_ptr and holds borrowed graphics-resource references.
updateWorld propagates matrices before cameras and rendering consume them. Avatar::attach classifies
OBJ groups and builds nine parts with local pivots. Model::LoadModel imports meshes and materials;
resolverRutaTextura resolves relative resources. PrepararRecursosSuperficie prepares planets on
demand.
Startup locates Models and Textures relative to the executable and its parent directories.
Window::Initialise centers the window in the primary monitor’s work area. The context is created before
GPU resources are loaded. Readable sources, the solution and local dependencies are included.

4. Transforms and lighting
SceneNode::local builds Mlocal = T · Ry · Rx · Rz · S. updateWorld computes Mworld = Mparent · Mlocal.
A part’s point is transformed locally and then inherits its parent’s transform. Rotation about a hinge p uses
T(p) · R(θ) · T(-p); Main.cpp applies this to the capsule and CoheteKeyframes::Dibujar to rocket parts.
shaders/shader_light.frag computes ambient, diffuse and Phong-style specular reflection: Id = kd ·
max(N·L,0) and Is = ks · max(V·R,0)^s. Point lights are attenuated with distance; spotlights add a cutoff
cone. Materials control shininess and specular intensity. Emission supplies self-brightness, rather than
illumination received by geometry.
The lighting cycle uses θ = 2πt/120 and sunHeight = sin(θ). A clamped day factor preserves ambient light
and controls lamps. F, L and K toggle three spotlights. Captures show deliberately dark areas; the
presence of a shader alone does not establish that lighting is free from defects.

Animation mathematics and testing
1. Rek’Sai
Opposing legs: 0.78 sin(φ) and 0.78 sin(φ+π). Arms, head and tail share that phase; φ advances with dt
and speed.
2. Keyframed rocket
20 channels; α=(t-ti)/(ti+1-ti); v=(1-α)vi+αvi+1. Includes translation, pivot rotation, staging and orbit.
3. Procedural rocket
Ascent h=Hτ², with τ clamped to [0,1]; ignition vibration and orbital oscillation.
4. Doors
Capsule: θ←θ+(θtarget-θ)min(1,6dt), around a local hinge. Hangar: clamped two-second opening.
5. Satellite panels
Factor interpolated toward 0/1, rotation toward ±85° and opposing oscillations when deployed.
6. Satellite trajectory
Two parametric lobes; a normalized tangent orients the object. θ←θ+ωdt; the guide adjusts parameter
steps using derivative magnitude.
7. Rival ships
d=(offset+18t) mod L. Accumulated distance selects a segment; position and tangent frame come from
track, surface or ocean.
Run LabCGeIHC.exe --probar-animaciones. The sequence injects events through the same GLFW callback used by the keyboard,
advances 3360 frames at dt=1/60 and captures every 120 frames. It activates 6 in rock, F4/1 for D1, Tab/4 for the hangar, Tab/3 for
the satellite, F2 for rivals and F1/W for gait. It does not replace manual review of framing, intersections or artistic quality.

8. Verification and reproducibility
Release x64 was compiled with the installed Visual Studio MSBuild. The build completed with existing
conversion warnings, mainly C4244/C4305. --probar-release completed space, rock, forest, ocean,
racing, D1 launch and hangar with exit code 0. The executable was tested from another directory. A real
window was observed and captured at the space base. Automatic captures document the zones’
framebuffers.
OBJ models are checked for in-range indices and finite values; missing fallback materials and discovered
absolute paths are corrected. These checks do not prove watertight meshes, absence of degenerate
faces or absence of all intersections. Optimization reduces detail; artistic quality and all collisions require
broader visual exploration. TARS is integrated, updated and conditionally visible in the ocean. The
Freezer-inspired station is loaded; this does not establish that every earlier ship variant remains in the
project.
Individual conclusion
Working on Rek’Sai connected geometry groups, pivots and matrices with visible motion. A gait requires
coordinated parts rather than unrelated leg rotations. Integration with input, cameras and HUD required a
clear separation between user intent, world state and presentation. I also learned that compilation alone
does not ensure that another person sees a window or finds the assets. Documenting the code and
repeating complete executable tests helps distinguish an implemented function from a verified
experience.
System conclusion and delivery limits
The scene combines transforms, synthetic cameras, lighting and procedural animation in a shared
application. Functional roles helped integrate models and experiences, but external resources and
outdated documentation caused discrepancies. The current preparation improves portability, size and
traceability without replacing outstanding tests. The evaluated version is in the individual
320324187_ProyectoFinal_GPO06 repository, on main. PROCEDENCIA.txt identifies the version of the
team project used for this copy. A second-computer test and full geometry review remain outstanding.
